# Publish checklist for the seven new posts

Everything below is a fact we do not have. Nothing on the live pages is
invented: each article is written *around* these gaps and says plainly when
something has not been measured. Filling them in makes the pages stronger; not
filling them in does not make anything on them wrong.

Ordered by how much it costs to leave alone.

---

## 1. Sitewide credibility, before anything else

These are not blog problems, but the blog now points at `/our-story` from
eleven pages, so they matter more than they did.

- [ ] **"42 farming families" and "seven generations" versus "founded 2024,
      one pair of hands."** Both appear across the site: the first in the
      footer, `/our-story`, `/contact` and `llms.txt`; the second in Rashmita's
      author bio on every post. As written they contradict each other. Pick
      the true version and use it everywhere. Author credibility carries real
      weight on health-adjacent topics, which is most of this journal.
- [ ] **"12,000+ homes"** on the home page hero. Real, or from the theme?
- [ ] **Product star ratings and review counts** on the shop and product
      cards. These are generated by `ratingFor()` in
      `src/components/sections/ProductsSection.tsx` from a hash of the slug.
      They are not real.

      One correction to the brief that flagged this: they are **not** in the
      JSON-LD. `productLd()` emits no `aggregateRating`, so there is no
      fabricated-markup exposure and no manual-action risk. It is a
      displayed-to-customers problem only, which is still worth fixing, but it
      is a smaller fire than the document assumed.

## 2. `/blog/wholesale-jaggery-nepal`

The page is publishable as written: it explains that we quote per enquiry
rather than publishing a rate card, and why. That is normal B2B practice and
it is honest. But it is a weaker page than it could be.

- [ ] Minimum order quantity
- [ ] Bulk price breaks by volume
- [ ] Bulk packaging options and sizes
- [ ] Lead time from order to dispatch
- [ ] Monthly production capacity
- [ ] Whether we can supply unbranded or white-label
- [ ] **Organic certification: do we hold one, and which?** The page currently
      says to ask us and that we will answer precisely. That is the safe
      wording. Do not upgrade it to a certification claim without the
      certificate, and note that the rest of the site says "100% organic"
      freely, which is a separate thing to look at.
- [ ] Whether we export, and where. The page assumes domestic only, because
      the rest of the site says pan-Nepal.

## 3. `/blog/himalayan-jaggery-sankhuwasabha` (the pillar)

- [ ] Our altitude, or the range across the terraces
- [ ] Our cane variety
- [ ] Harvest and crushing months
- [ ] Yield per ropani
- [ ] **The exact clarification method.** The article says we skim by hand and
      add nothing to speed it up, and that the full method is being written
      down properly rather than described from memory. That paragraph is a
      promise; keep it.

## 4. `/blog/sakhar-price-in-nepal`

- [ ] Current Kathmandu retail range for Terai gur. The article deliberately
      prints no competitor price and explains why, which is defensible, but a
      checked range would make the comparison land harder.
- [ ] Our harvest and dispatch calendar, for the seasonality section.
- [ ] **This page carries a visible "Prices last updated: August 2026" line.**
      When prices change, update the price table, that line, and the FAQ
      answers that repeat the figures, together.

## 5. `/blog/chaku-recipe-maghe-sankranti`

- [ ] Whether our block or our powder actually makes better chaku. The article
      says the block, explains the reasoning (lower moisture, predictable
      set), and labels it a cook's opinion rather than a tested result. Make a
      batch of each and the sentence can be upgraded.
- [ ] **Timing.** This must stay live and be photographed well before
      mid-January. Search engines need weeks to rank a seasonal page ahead of
      its peak. It is up now, which is early, which is right. Refresh it each
      year rather than rewriting it.

## 6. The three Nepali posts

- [ ] **Have a native Nepali speaker read all three before promoting them.**
      They are written directly in Nepali rather than translated from English
      drafts, and the terminology and register were chosen deliberately, but a
      native read is the difference between "clearly written by someone" and
      "clearly written by something." These posts target readers who will
      notice.
- [ ] Confirm the Devanagari spelling choices, particularly सख्खर against
      alternatives, and whether the Romanised forms in the first paragraphs
      match how people actually type them into Google.

## 7. Photography

Every new post has a `README.md` shot list in `public/blog/<slug>/`.

All seven currently use a real product photograph from `/products/` as an
interim hero, with alt text describing that photograph honestly rather than
the one in the shot list. Several share the same image, so the social cards
look alike until the real shots land.

The two highest-value images on the whole list:

- [ ] `himalayan-jaggery-kadhai-wood-fire.jpg` — the boil, with firelight. This
      is the picture the entire brand argument rests on.
- [ ] The six chaku step shots. Recipe pages without step photography do not
      win the image carousel, and the carousel is where the clicks are.
